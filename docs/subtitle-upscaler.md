# The subtitle upscaler: ArtCNN on the Neural Engine

Status: written 2026-10-25 on `research/12bit-fel`. The upscaler is built, tested against a Float32 reference and
measured on the Apple TV 4K (A15, tvOS 27.2). Nothing in the engine calls it yet; the integration (part 6) is open.
All figures are Release builds on the A15 unless they say otherwise; Debug figures are not comparable (the planner
alone ran 50 times slower there).

## Part 1. What it does

`SubtitleUpscaler` upscales a bitmap subtitle (PGS, DVB, DVD: an object's premultiplied RGBA crop) 2x with ArtCNN
(https://github.com/Artoriuz/ArtCNN, MIT; `ArtCNNLicense`). ArtCNN is single channel, so the picture is split into its
alpha and its premultiplied luma, each runs through a network, and the colour comes back as the source's chroma,
interpolated bilinearly and scaled by the new alpha, clamped to [0, 1] and [0, alpha]. Output farther than 5 source
pixels (S, the support) from content is exactly transparent. Other sizes take one or two 2x passes and a Lanczos-3
resample. Configurations: C4F16, C4F32, balanced (C4F16 on alpha, C4F32 on luma).

The network runs on the Neural Engine through Core ML. GPU passes prepare its input and composite its output: the
content mask (one plane, the support, over the kept area), each window's gather, each window's composite, and clears
of everything no window covers.

## Part 2. How a picture is run

1. **Plan** (`SubtitleUpscaleTilePlan`, CPU, 0.1 to 0.7 ms). The caller's `Content` gives each row's first and last
   opaque column (the `CGImage` and pixel buffer conveniences scan it). The rows are cut into bands, each one core
   height, covered by cores across only the columns their own support spans; rows with no support take no band. A
   dynamic programme over the rows picks the bands, each band's columns are the cheapest cover of its span, and every
   window is costed by its measured pair-call time.
2. **Windows.** The models have fixed shapes: heights 46, 78, 142 by widths 256, 512, 1024, 2048, each a core plus
   the network's 7-pixel reach on every side, so a core's output is exact.
3. **One submission, no CPU waits between stages.** The gather command buffer (clears, mask, then each window's
   gather in its own encoder followed by a GPU-signalled event) and the composite command buffer (each window's
   composite after a wait on its own event) are committed before any prediction runs.
4. **Predictions in flight.** One thread per window waits on its gather event, runs one Core ML call for both planes
   (a pair program: `xa`, `xl` in, `ya`, `yl` out), and signals its composite's event. Every event is signalled
   whatever fails.
5. **Zero copy.** Each window's four planes are IOSurface-backed `OneComponent16Half` pixel buffers, wrapped once as
   `r16Float` textures and once as `MLMultiArray(pixelBuffer:)`; the inputs and output backings are made once per
   surface set. A prediction that does not write its backing is an error.

Models: one multifunction package per alpha / luma pairing, a function per window shape, weights stored once
(`ArtCNNCoreMLModels`, regenerated by `Scripts/ArtCNN`). They load with the `fastPrediction` specialisation.

## Part 3. Costs (A15)

One-time, at app open (`prewarm(_:)`, then `prepare(canvas:)` on the upscaler that will run):

| | |
|---|---|
| GPU pipelines | 5 to 10 ms (about 1 s when the system shader cache is cold) |
| Neural Engine, one pairing, cold (install, OS update, purged cache) | about 12 s, nearly all function loads |
| the same, warm | about 0.5 s |
| `prepare(canvas:)` | 25 to 90 ms |

Per picture, steady state, p50 (p99 within about 1 ms):

| Picture | C4F16 | balanced | C4F32 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 900x80, one line | 3.7 ms | 4.3 ms | 5.0 ms |
| 1400x120 strip | 3.7 | 6.8 | 8.3 |
| 1500x150, two lines of different lengths | 6.9 | 7.6 | 8.0 |
| 1920x200 strip | 7.6 | 10.6 | 12.2 |

Of a call, about 70 to 75% is the Neural Engine. The first picture on a prepared upscaler is still 1 to 4 ms above
steady state on some pictures (open). C4F16 alone is the least steady: its predictions vary run to run more than the
others'.

How it got here, the 1920x200 strip on balanced: 23 ms (Neural Engine, fixed tiles, Debug build) and 16 ms (the same,
Release), 13.9 ms with per-band planning and one call per window, 11.1 ms with the stages chained by events and the
predictions in flight, 10.6 ms with each window predicting as its own gather is written.

## Part 4. Findings

- **The GPU network went.** On the A15 the Neural Engine was faster in every configuration but C4F16 on small
  pictures (even there), its p99 within about a millisecond of its median where the GPU's reached 1.5 times it, and it
  leaves the GPU to the video.
- **Core ML shapes.** A flexible shape is fast only at its default size; with infrequent reshapes each change
  re-optimises for about 10 ms. Fixed shapes in a multifunction package switch for free.
- **Per-pixel cost is near the Neural Engine's ceiling** for C4F32 (about 6 TFLOPS of fp16); C4F16's 16 channels use
  it less well. Repacking pixels into channels would add 4x the work as zero weights; the A15 gains nothing from int8.
- **No hand-over cost.** The same burst of predictions times the same after a GPU write into its surfaces, back to
  back, and on Core ML's own arrays; zero copy is kept on principle and costs nothing.
- **The CPU's clock matters, QoS does not.** The predict stage was a third shorter with another core kept busy;
  utility, default and user-interactive QoS made no difference. Removing the CPU's waits (part 2, steps 3 and 4)
  took most of that.
- **A richer shape library is worth 3 to 5%** (80 functions against 12, modelled over 100 subtitle objects), less
  than its load time.
- **Core AI (tvOS 27) cannot help on the A15**: `ComputeUnitKind.availableKinds` is `[cpu]` there; preferring the
  Neural Engine fails to specialise, and the CPU runs ArtCNN at about 30 ns a pixel. Ahead-of-time compilation is
  documented only for devices with Apple Intelligence. Revisit on newer Apple TV hardware.
- **Metal 4's machine-learning pass** builds an MPSGraph package (`metal-package-builder`), most likely GPU work;
  not measured.

## Part 5. Open

- The first picture after `prepare(canvas:)`: the remaining 1 to 4 ms.
- Single-window pictures cannot overlap their stages; a split worth its extra call could be chosen by the planner.
- The plan's CPU time (up to 0.7 ms) is on the critical path.
- Under playback load (4K HDR decode, the GPU finisher, FEL composition) nothing is measured yet.

## Part 6. Integration (not built)

The overlay drain already decodes bitmap display sets about 60 s ahead of the playhead (`SubtitleOverlayDrainer`),
one `SubtitleCue` per object with its cropped `CGImage`. The plan: where the drain applies decoded cues, image cues go
to a prepared upscaler on a background queue, nearest the playhead first, and enter `subtitleCues` once upscaled (a
cue is late by its upscale only after a seek or at start). Results are keyed by stream, start time, position and
pixel size, so a re-decoded cue reuses its upscale. The scale is the display's pixels over the canvas's (a 1080p
canvas on a 2160p panel is 2x; a 1080p panel needs none). The drain's apply step was being reworked when this was
written, so the hook waits for it.
